### Started { date }

For as long as I can remember, I've always wanted to make games. In fact, the main reason I went into the Computer Science major is because I wanted to be a game programmer. Even with that experience under my belt, it's proved to be quite difficult to finish my first game. There are a variety of reasons for this: having trouble splitting up tasks into bite-sized pieces, not having many ideas that interest me, etc. 

With the help of my sibling, I recently came to the realization that a big reason why I struggle to finish my first game is because I don't play short indie games very often. There is a fundamental disconnect between what I want to make and the content I am feeding my brain; if I play games with large scopes made by multi-billion dollar companies all the time, where am I going to get the inspiration to make a small game made by one or two people?

This is my first step in changing that. This blog will be a collection of my thoughts on short indie games of all kinds. I don't have a formal structure for these notes, but generally I will write about the things that jumped out at me and how I can take inspiration from it for my own games.

## Escaped Chasm - Temmie Chang

My sibling has been telling me to play this game for almost three years at this point. The [itch.io](https://tuyoki.itch.io/escaped-chasm) page has been rotting in my Chrome tabs for equally as long, long enough that it actually reminded me of the manner the main character of this game deteriorates as the game progresses.
